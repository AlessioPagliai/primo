# bonegen — generated bones for primo

Handoff for the AI session on the simulation workstation. Goal set by the user: **all the bones of the robot generated, and the robot walking in Isaac Lab with them**, with the loads taken from the simulation, not assumed.

Idea: the motors stay where the CAD puts them. A bone is whatever connects the screw holes of two motors without touching anything that moves. It is generated by topology optimisation from: the motors, their screw holes, the volumes swept by the neighbours over the joint range, the screw head and tool paths, and the loads. Change a motor → change one entry → regenerate.

Mounting is the standard one for every motor (user decision): **one bone on the body holes (outer), the other bone on the output holes (inner), both on the output face.** No back mounting.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `bonegen.py` | the pipeline, one file: voxelise → pads / keep-outs / screw paths → SIMP optimisation with several load cases → smooth mesh of the same volume → exact pads, holes, counterbores and motors cut with booleans → `report.json` |
| `thigh_free.json` | pilot, **free mode**: no CAD bone as input, only motors + holes + keep-outs + loads |
| `thigh.json` | same bone in CAD-interface mode (copies the attachment zones from the CAD femur). Used only to measure the current femur as baseline. Not the way forward: the copied zones ate 97 of the 187 cm³ |
| `render.py` | small renderer for comparison pictures, no GPU needed |
| `results/` | the 4 mm pilot: generated thigh (`final.stl`, millimetres, robot root frame), `report.json`, `thigh_compare.png` |

Meshes: the configs read the robot STL (metres, root frame, zero pose) from `sim/meshes` of the repository (`mesh_dir`). Known defect of the pilot mesh: a few needle-like sliver triangles from the smoothing; clean them before printing.

Setup: `python -m venv .venv`, `pip install numpy scipy pyamg scikit-image trimesh manifold3d`.
Run: `python bonegen.py thigh_free.json --pitch 4 --iters 70`; `--remesh` redoes check and meshes from a saved density; `--find-holes motor.stl y@-120:-112` lists the round holes of a mesh inside a slab.

## Config in short

- `envelope_mm` box where material may exist; `volume_cm3` mass budget (187.2 = current femur, solid PA-CF as the URDF assumes).
- `interfaces` of type `pads`: `axis`, `face` (coordinate of the motor's mounting face), `side` (which way the bone grows), `holes` (centres in the two other axes), `pad_d`, `pad_t`, `hole_d`, `head_d`, `head_depth` (counterbore), `access_len` (straight tool path kept empty), optional `ring` joining the pads. Pads are solid, holes / counterbores / tool paths are empty, both exact in the final mesh.
- `keepout_static` bodies that move with the bone. `sweeps` bodies that turn past it: swept over `range` (or `use_range`) in 3° steps, grown by `clearance_mm`. The report says if a pad lies inside a swept volume (`interface_inside_swept_volume_mm3`).
- `load_cases` wrenches at `load_centre` spread on the load pads; the other interface is fixed. Objective = sum of compliance / compliance of the current bone, so every case counts the same.

## What the pilot showed (Mac, 4 mm, ASSUMED loads — indicative only)

- The current CAD femur is a flat 10 mm plate. The knee of the CAD: femur plate y −129.4…−139.4 on the body holes, shank plate from −141.5 on a Ø50 hub through the femur ring. Gap 2.1 mm: **screw heads of the femur must be counterbored** or they hit the shank. Done in `thigh_free.json` (M4, 4.5 mm deep).
- Range of motion: **the current CAD femur touches the shank assembly from about 123° of knee flexion**; the URDF limit is 165° (inherited from the reference robot). The pilot keeps 0–120° free. The walking policy uses −5…116°. The real limits must be decided joint by joint.
- Free mode, same mass, forces on the leg line (y = −127), stiffness of the generated thigh against the CAD femur: lateral bending ×1.75, torsion ×1.47, knee-motor torque ×0.92, fore-aft ×0.95, axial ×0.59 (in absolute terms 0.14 → 0.24 mm under twice the body weight). In words: the flat plate is already efficient in its own plane and weak sideways and in twist; the generated shape trades a little of the first for a lot of the second. Overall a draw at this resolution. Picture: `thigh_compare.png` (current left, generated right; neighbours in blue, motors in grey, bone seen from above on the right of each group).
- At 4 mm the surface is lumpy and thin members are unreliable. Real results need 2 mm.

## Plan for the full robot

1. **Loads from the simulation.** Play the walking policy (`Isaac-Velocity-R5-KneeHard-Play-v0`; `knee_probe.py` shows how to patch `play.py`). For every joint log per step the 6D wrench the joint transmits (`robot.root_physx_view.get_link_incoming_joint_force()`, check frame and sign once with a static pose where the answer is known: robot hanging, then standing) and `robot.data.applied_torque`. Include turning, stairs, stops, and a few pushes. Per bone build 6–10 load cases: the step where each force and moment component peaks (use the 99.5th percentile, contact spikes are partly solver artefacts), plus a fall/impact case and the static worst cases. Confidence: good for walking loads, poor for impacts — keep a safety factor and say so.
2. **Bones to generate** (printed parts that are pure connections): hip pitch→roll bracket, hip roll→yaw bracket, femur, tibia (two ankle motors and pushrods inside: many keep-outs), upper arm, forearm, shoulder brackets. Keep from CAD for now: pelvis, torso, foot, ankle gimbal and cranks.
3. **Neighbours.** A bone's keep-out is the swept volume of its neighbours. Round 1 uses the CAD neighbours. Round 2 regenerates each bone against the generated neighbours. Then a clash check of every pair over the joint ranges (same `swept()` lookup) before anything goes to the simulator.
4. **Resolution.** 2–2.5 mm. CPU (scipy + pyamg) works but takes hours per bone. Faster: replace `Grid.solve` by a matrix-free conjugate gradient on the GPU (PyTorch float64: gather `u[edof]`, multiply by `KE * E_e`, `index_add_` back, Jacobi preconditioner, warm start). Nothing else changes.
5. **Back into Isaac Lab.** Mass, centre of mass and inertia of each generated bone (trimesh, density 1.25 g/cm³) → into the URDF in place of the CAD values, generated STL as visual meshes (left side by mirroring) → `convert_urdf.py --merge-joints` → fine-tune KneeHard → record the video → log the loads again → one more round of bones if they changed much. Isaac Lab cannot optimise bones while walking (links are rigid, there is no stress inside them): the loop runs in rounds.
6. **Then lighter.** Once stiffness is matched with real loads, run `--vol 0.8` and `0.7`.
7. **Printing.** FDM PA-CF is weak between layers: minimum member ≥ 5 mm, print orientation with the main load paths in the layers, or MJF/SLS for these parts. Material data from printed coupons before any safety factor is claimed.

## Rules

Never present an assumed load or modulus as measured. Interfaces exactly as the motors require. English only, assistants are called "AI".
